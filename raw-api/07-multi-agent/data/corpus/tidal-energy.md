# Tidal energy

Tidal energy converts the motion of ocean tides into electricity. Tides are
driven by gravity, so the resource is predictable years in advance, unlike wind
or solar.

- Capacity factor for a tidal stream turbine is typically 30 to 40 percent.
- A tidal barrage holds back water at high tide and releases it through turbines.
- Tidal stream turbines sit on the seabed and spin as the current flows past.
- The strongest tidal currents occur in narrow channels and around headlands.
- Output is intermittent within a day but follows a fixed, repeating cycle.
- Saltwater corrosion and biofouling are the main maintenance challenges.
